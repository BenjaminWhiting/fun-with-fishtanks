Behind the prints

How it's made

Every part here is code, every file is checked before it reaches the printer, and the printer is driven over my home network. Here's who does what, the path from an idea to a piece of plastic, and the mistakes that put the checks there.

Who does what

The AI is Claude, working on my PC through Claude Code. It writes the models, runs the checks, slices the files and can talk to the printer. It reads a short list of standing rules at the start of every session, and that list is in the repo too.

My side is smaller and can't be handed over. I say what I want. I hold test pieces against the real tank. I look at the pictures and say what's wrong with them. I check that the plate is empty. And nothing prints until I say go for that exact file.

From an idea to plastic

  1. 1. Talk
    I describe the part. When an idea changes direction, the AI says back what it understood and asks a few deciding questions before it models anything.
  2. 2. Model
    Each part is an OpenSCAD file with its sizes as settings at the top. Rendered mock-ups come first, then the version that can actually print.
  3. 3. Look for overhangs
    printcheck.py slices the model every 0.2 mm and compares each layer with the one below, reporting anything that would start in mid-air.
  4. 4. Slice
    slice.py runs Bambu Studio from the command line, with settings that have no start or finish tune.
  5. 5. Preflight
    preflight.py checks the file is intact and sliced for the A1 mini, follows every move (arcs included) to make sure it stays inside what the printer can reach, and checks the material and that the printer is idle.
  6. 6. Clear plate
    I check the plate is empty. The printer has a camera, but black plastic on a dark plate at night is hard to judge, so a person looks.
  7. 7. Go
    On my go, bambu.py uploads the file and starts it over the local network.

The scripts are in the tools folder, with setup notes in its README.

What went wrong, and what it changed

  • Check every move, not just the partA file once carried another printer's start-up moves, reaching far past the 7-inch bed. It was stopped in time. Now preflight follows every move before a file is sent.
  • Find the cause before trying againAfter a start-up fault, a quick restart without anyone looking at the printer sent the toolhead into the nozzle wiper. Now nothing restarts until the cause is found.
  • Thin parts climb on their ownKnee braces printed upside down grew as free-standing 45-degree sticks and snapped off. Anything that rises alone needs real thickness.
  • A brim fills nearby holesA brim around a part that prints on a face full of sockets sealed every one of them with a thin film. Parts with holes near the edge print without a brim.
  • Test the fitLantern pegs with 0.15 mm of play came out loose. Small fit tests with several amounts of play now come before big parts.
  • A screen isn't a handA ring half an inch wide looked fine in a render and far too chunky in real life. A cat under an inch tall was the same lesson the other way round.
  • A slicer that hangsThe command-line slicer froze forever on a hidden warning dialog from a sound driver. The slicing script now clicks it away.
  • Print files remember where they came fromThe slicer saves each model's full folder path, user name included, inside the print file. A script takes those out before anything is published.
